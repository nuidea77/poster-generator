# Mongolian culture for visuals

## Calendar of occasions

| When | Occasion | Visual cues |
|---|---|---|
| Dec–Jan | Deep winter, New Year (Шинэ жил) | Snow, frost, warm interiors, lights, fir tree, gifts |
| Jan–Feb (lunar, **date changes every year — never assume it**) | **Tsagaan Sar** (Цагаан сар), the biggest holiday | Ul boov stacks, buuz, deel, khadag (blue silk scarf), family greeting elders, warm light; white = purity |
| 8 March | International Women's Day | Flowers, elegance, warm pastel palette |
| Mar–May | Spring, young livestock season, dust storms | Fresh green shoots arriving late (May), lambs, windy light |
| 1 June | Children's Day | Bright primary colours, play, parks |
| 11–13 July | **Naadam** | Wrestling, horse racing, archery, steppe, blue sky, festival tents; keep logos modest |
| Jul–Aug | Summer, countryside, airag | Steppe, gers, horses, long golden evenings |
| Sep–Oct | Autumn, back to school, preparing for winter | Golden larch forests, harvest, school |
| Nov | Early winter | First snow, cosy indoor scenes |

Also: Constitution Day, Independence Day, local soum anniversaries. When the brief names a lunar date, trust the brief over memory.

## Colour symbolism

- **Blue (хөх)** — eternal sky, the most loved colour; khadag is usually blue.
- **White (цагаан)** — purity, milk, good beginnings; central to Tsagaan Sar.
- **Red (улаан)** — fire, energy, celebration.
- **Yellow/gold (шар)** — prosperity, religion in some contexts.
- **Black (хар)** — mourning, bad luck; avoid black-dominated designs for celebrations and family occasions (dark premium styling for products is fine).

Numbers: 9 (есөн эрдэнэ) is auspicious, 3 and 7 good; 13 is not taboo.

## Depicting people and tradition respectfully

- Show people modern and natural by default; traditional deel only when the occasion is traditional.
- Ger interiors: the khoimor (back, place of honour) for elders; the hearth (голомт) is sacred — never show stepping over fire or throwing things into it.
- Greeting at Tsagaan Sar: the younger person supports the elder's elbows from below.
- Respect for elders and nature (springs, ovoo, mountains) — no littering, trampling or mockery in scenes.
- Do not reduce Mongolia to clichés (eagles, endless steppe) unless the brief is about them.

## Never improvise

- **State symbols** (state emblem, flag, state Soyombo) — banned in commercial advertising.
- **Soyombo, traditional ornaments (хээ угалз), Mongolian script calligraphy** — image models distort them; use only artwork supplied by the client, as a reference.
- Real people, historical figures, religious imagery and banknotes must not be used in a disparaging or commercial way.
