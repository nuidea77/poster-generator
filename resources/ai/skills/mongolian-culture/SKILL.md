# Mongolian culture for visuals

## Calendar of occasions

| When | Occasion | Visual cues |
|---|---|---|
| Dec–Jan | Deep winter, New Year (Шинэ жил) | Snow, frost, warm interiors, lights, fir tree, gifts |
| Jan–Feb (lunar, **date changes every year — never assume it**) | **Tsagaan Sar** (Цагаан сар), the biggest holiday | Ul boov stacks, buuz, deel, khadag (blue silk scarf), family greeting elders, warm light; white = purity |
| 8 March | International Women's Day | Flowers, elegance, warm pastel palette |
| Mar–May | Spring, young livestock season, dust storms | Fresh green shoots arriving late (May), lambs, windy light |
| 1 June | Children's Day | Bright primary colours, play, parks |
| 11–13 July | **Naadam** | Wrestling, horse racing, archery, steppe, blue sky, festival tents; keep logos modest |
| Jul–Aug | Summer, countryside, airag | Steppe, gers, horses, long golden evenings |
| Sep–Oct | Autumn, back to school, preparing for winter | Golden larch forests, harvest, school |
| Nov | Early winter | First snow, cosy indoor scenes |

Also: Constitution Day, Independence Day, local soum anniversaries. When the brief names a lunar date, trust the brief over memory.

## Colour symbolism

- **Blue (хөх)** — eternal sky, the most loved colour; khadag is usually blue.
- **White (цагаан)** — purity, milk, good beginnings; central to Tsagaan Sar.
- **Red (улаан)** — fire, energy, celebration.
- **Yellow/gold (шар)** — prosperity, religion in some contexts.
- **Black (хар)** — mourning, bad luck; avoid black-dominated designs for celebrations and family occasions (dark premium styling for products is fine).

Numbers: 9 (есөн эрдэнэ) is auspicious, 3 and 7 good; 13 is not taboo.

## Depicting people and tradition respectfully

- Show people modern and natural by default; traditional deel only when the occasion is traditional.
- Ger interiors: the khoimor (back, place of honour) for elders; the hearth (голомт) is sacred — never show stepping over fire or throwing things into it.
- Greeting at Tsagaan Sar: the younger person supports the elder's elbows from below.
- Respect for elders and nature (springs, ovoo, mountains) — no littering, trampling or mockery in scenes.
- Do not reduce Mongolia to clichés (eagles, endless steppe) unless the brief is about them.

## Tsagaan Sar table (идээ засал) — get it right
Image models do not know the Tsagaan Sar table: they draw croissants, puff pastry, cakes or a Chinese New Year spread. Describe every item concretely in the prompt and forbid the wrong ones.

| Item | What it really is | Prompt wording (English) |
|---|---|---|
| **Ул боов** (хэвийн боов) — the centrepiece | Flat, dense, oblong biscuits pressed in carved wooden moulds, so each has an embossed ornament on top; smooth matte surface, pale golden to light brown; stacked on a large plate in an **odd number of tiers** (3, 5, 7; 9 for elders) | "a tall stack of flat rectangular Mongolian ul boov biscuits — dense, smooth, matte pale-golden shortbread pieces, each with a carved geometric ornament (knot and scroll shapes, not letters) embossed on top, stacked in seven neat tiers on a large plate; NOT puff pastry, NOT croissants, NOT layered or flaky" |
| **Идээ** on top of the boov | White dairy: **ааруул** (dried curd pieces), **өрөм** (clotted cream), ээзгий; plus sugar cubes and wrapped candies | "crowned with white dried curd pieces (aaruul), a slice of clotted cream, sugar cubes and a few wrapped candies" |
| **Шүүс / уц** | A whole boiled sheep back with the broad fat tail, pale and steamed, on its own large platter (sometimes with the head beside it) | "a whole boiled lamb back with its broad fat tail (uts), pale, steaming, on a large oval platter" — keep it appetising and tasteful, no gore |
| **Сүүтэй цай** in a **мөнгөн аяга** | Milky salted tea in a silver bowl (engraved, often on a small silver stand), served with both hands, often over a blue khadag | "milky Mongolian tea in an engraved silver bowl on a small stand, resting on a folded blue silk khadag" |
| **Бууз** | Steamed meat dumplings, pleated, round with a small open top | "a plate of steamed buuz dumplings with pleated tops" |
| Хадаг, хөөрөг | Blue silk ceremonial scarf; small snuff bottle exchanged in greetings | "a blue silk khadag", "a small stone snuff bottle" |

- Setting: warm light, wooden or felt-covered low table, ger interior or a modern home with a deel; white and blue dominate.
- **Never**: red lanterns, dragons, Chinese characters, red envelopes, fireworks, chopsticks (Chinese New Year confusion); croissants, puff pastry, layer cakes, cupcakes; alcohol in ads; shiny plastic-looking food.
- After generating, check the boov first: if it looks flaky, puffy or croissant-like, regenerate with the wording above. If the embossing looks like letters or fake writing, that is acceptable only when it is small and soft; obvious pseudo-text → regenerate with "abstract knot ornament, no letters".

## Mongolian ornaments (хээ угалз)
Real ornaments are symmetrical, complete and precise. Use them as **borders, corner pieces, frames or a faint background**, never under text and never cut mid-shape.

| Ornament | Look | Meaning / use |
|---|---|---|
| **Өлзий хээ** | Endless knot, interlaced loops in a square | Eternity, good fortune — centre medallion or corner |
| **Түмэн өлзий** | Continuous meander / key-fret border | Unbroken happiness — the classic border band |
| **Үүлэн хээ** | Rounded cloud scrolls | Sky, blessing — light, airy borders |
| **Эвэр хээ** | Paired ram-horn spirals | Strength, prosperous herds |
| **Хамар хээ** | Mirrored S-curve scroll (nose pattern) | Classic decorative band |
| **Галын хээ** | Stylised flames | Hearth, vitality |

- Colours: blue, red, white, gold on felt, wood (orange ger doors), deel trim; on posters tone-on-tone gold on deep blue or red, or white on blue.
- Prompt: "a symmetrical traditional Mongolian ornament border (tumen-ulzii meander band with ulzii endless-knot corners), precise geometric linework, tone-on-tone gold on deep blue, only around the edges". Add "no letters, no script".
- Not on discount junk or mocking contexts; the Soyombo and the state emblem are **not** ornaments (see below). Avoid the хас (swastika) motif in ads — it is misread abroad.

## Mongolian script (монгол бичиг)
Vertical, written top to bottom, columns left to right; letters join and change shape by position.
- **Image models cannot write it.** They produce fake squiggles that readers spot at once — worse than nothing. Every visual prompt keeps "no text, no letters, no script".
- Real Mongolian script only from the client's own artwork (as a reference to keep exact), or set in code with a proper font when the studio supports it — never invented.
- Cyrillic text (via `add_text`) is the normal way to carry the message.

## Never improvise

- **State symbols** (state emblem, flag, state Soyombo) — banned in commercial advertising.
- **Soyombo and Mongolian script calligraphy** — image models distort them; use only artwork supplied by the client, as a reference. Ornaments (хээ угалз) are fine as decoration when prompted as above; a specific ornament the client owns (brand pattern) comes from their file.
- Real people, historical figures, religious imagery and banknotes must not be used in a disparaging or commercial way.
