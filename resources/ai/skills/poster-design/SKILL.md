# Poster design

The delivered image is the whole ad: picture **and** message. A good poster is **designed**, not assembled: one idea, one dominant element, a few supporting ones, and space that is planned. A bad one is a stock photo with labels stacked on it.

## 1. Why our posters looked cheap (avoid all of these)
| Failure | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Stock photo + labels** | smiling model on a blurred office/clinic, text stacked in the empty left half | art-direct the visual (§3): a colour world, a graphic idea, a crop with intent |
| **Box on everything** | kicker pill, headline box, offer pill, date chip, CTA pill, contacts strip — 6 boxes | **at most 2 filled shapes**: usually the offer and the CTA. Everything else is plain type on a calm area |
| **Flat hierarchy** | headline, offer, checklist and CTA all medium size | one element is **3–6× larger** than the rest; at most 3 other sizes |
| **Same template every time** | top-left stack → CTA pill → contacts | choose a composition map (§4) that fits *this* picture and message |
| **Dead and crowded zones** | empty middle, everything squeezed into the top and bottom | space is intentional: either generous negative space around the hero, or a full, rich layout — never a hole |
| **Text ignores the picture** | headline floating over a random area | type and image interlock: headline beside the face, along the product's edge, in a colour field painted for it |
| **Too many words** | 4 benefit lines + 3 badges + contacts | hook + offer + CTA + contacts; the rest goes to the caption |
| **Font chaos** | 3+ fonts, a display face for facts | ≤ 2 fonts: one headline face + `sans` for the rest |
| **Mixed alignment** | centred headline, left checklist, contacts stuck in a corner | one axis for the whole content block |
| **Crammed edges** | type and icons touching the frame, badges glued to the product | ≥ 7% margin on every side; air between the product and its labels |
| **Low contrast** | thin white type over a busy photo, grey on dark | a scrim, a darker/lighter area, or a solid shape behind it; dark on light, light on dark |
| **Colour noise** | neon pink + yellow + red on a clinic poster | 60-30-10, 2–3 colours, matched to the sector's mood |

## 2. The big idea first
Before any prompt, write one sentence: **"The poster shows ___ so that the viewer feels ___; the eye goes ___ → ___ → ___."**
Pick one hero: the product, a person's emotion, a bold number (`-40%`, `25 жил`, `1 ХОНОГ`), or a symbol (a rose for a rock band's anniversary). Everything else supports it.

## 2b. Design fundamentals
**Hierarchy and reading path.** The eye enters at the focal point, then follows a path — top-left → bottom-right (Z), or centre → out. Order the blocks along it: hook → hero → offer → action → contacts. Size, colour and isolation make something important; make only one thing loudest.

**Colour harmony** — choose one scheme deliberately and name it in the prompt:
| Scheme | Feel | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Monochromatic (one hue, many tones) | calm, premium, coherent | navy → cobalt → ice blue, white type |
| Analogous (neighbours) | warm, natural, food, lifestyle | terracotta, amber, cream |
| Complementary (opposites) | energy, sale, sport — use one as accent only | deep blue world + orange offer |
| Split-complementary | lively but balanced | teal + coral + peach |
| Neutral + one accent | editorial, luxury, minimal | black/white/stone + one red word |
Topic colours as a starting point, never a rule: health → clean white + one confident green/blue; rock/night → black + red/amber; kids → bright primaries; luxury → black, cream, gold; eco → greens, kraft. Brand colours win. Contrast: type against what is behind it must be strong (white/yellow on dark, near-black on light); never mid-tone on mid-tone. Digital posters are RGB (sRGB JPEG); print CMYK is not our concern.

**60-30-10:** about 60% dominant colour (the background world), 30% secondary (the subject, panels), 10% accent (the offer, the CTA, the starred word). Triadic schemes (three evenly spaced hues) are lively but hard — keep one dominant and the other two small. At most 2–3 colours plus neutrals.

**Reading patterns:** a poster with little text reads in a **Z** (top-left → top-right → diagonal → bottom-left → bottom-right): headline top, hero across, offer and action at the bottom right. Text-heavy layouts read in an **F** down the left edge — keep their lines left-aligned.

**Gestalt — how the eye groups things:**
- **Proximity:** what belongs together sits close (kicker on the headline, price on the product name); separate groups get a clear gap.
- **Similarity:** the same job gets the same look (all benefit lines the same size and icon; one button style).
- **Closure:** a partly drawn shape (a cut circle, a cropped arch, a subject cut by the frame) is completed by the eye — tension and elegance without more elements.
- **Figure-ground:** the hero and the type must separate clearly from the background — by tone, colour, blur or a scrim.

**Composition and grid.**
- **Rule of thirds:** put the face, the eye or the product on a third-line crossing, not dead centre (unless the map is symmetric by intent).
- **Grid:** every text block starts on the same margin (≈ 6–8% of the width) and shares one or two column edges; gaps between groups are equal multiples. Ask the visual to respect the same margins.
- **Balance:** symmetric (centred, formal, premium, events) or asymmetric (big subject one side, type the other — more dynamic). A heavy dark subject on one side needs a strong type block on the other.

**Negative space.** Empty space is an element: it frames the hero and makes a poster look premium. Leave real air around the headline and the product; don't fill every corner with a badge, icon or line. Planned emptiness ≠ a dead hole between unrelated blocks.

**Imagery quality and blending.** The visual is generated at full resolution; never upscale a tiny crop. The subject must belong to its background: same light direction, colour cast and shadow; edges blended, not pasted. When you combine a product photo with a new scene, ask for matching light, a contact shadow and reflections ("the product sits naturally in the scene, lit by the same warm window light, soft contact shadow").

## 3. Art-direct the visual (the image model's job)
The image carries the design — colour world, shapes, light, space for the type. Never ask for "a photo of X with space for text". Ask for a **designed poster background**:
- **Colour world:** 1 dominant colour (brand or mood) + 1 neutral + 1 accent, named in the prompt ("deep cobalt #1E3A8A world, warm white, one coral accent"). The scene, props, wardrobe and light follow it.
- **Graphic structure painted in:** colour blocks, a big circle or arch behind the subject, a diagonal split, a frame, a spotlight pool, a gradient field, a textured paper band — painted by the model, text-free. These give the type a home and make it look designed.
- **Crop with intent:** tight crops (face from the eyes down, product filling 60% of the height, hands in action), low or high angles, the subject cut by the frame edge. Avoid centred, medium, eye-level stock framing.
- **Light:** one clear light idea — hard sun with shadows, studio rim light on black, soft window light, neon, golden hour. Light makes it premium.
- **People:** real, specific, styled in the colour world (wardrobe in the brand colour), an expression that matches the message, looking toward the headline or the viewer. No generic smiling-at-camera stock.
- **Product:** hero scale (35–60% of the height), real contact shadow, a surface and props that tell the story (ingredients, use, setting).
- **Space for type:** name the zone and what fills it: "the left 40% is a flat cobalt field", "the top third is dark smoky stage air".
- End with: "no text, no letters, no numbers, no logos".

Prompt formula: **[hero + action/expression] · [crop/angle] · [colour world with hex] · [graphic structure] · [light] · [type zone: where and how calm] · [style reference: e.g. Nike campaign, Apple product shot, Kinfolk editorial, Korean beauty ad] · no text.**

## 4. Composition maps (choose one per poster; vary across a series)

```
│ ██████████│      │LINE │ 🧍 │      │ ╭──────╮ │      │   hero   │
│  subject │      │offer│    │      │ │ hero │ │      │  image   │

│    📦    │      │    ╱     │      │ ▢  ▢  ▢  │      │  mood    │
│  price   │      │  ╱ offer │      │ caption… │      │ HEADLINE │
│   CTA    │      │╱   CTA   │      │   CTA    │      │ small CTA│
│ contacts │      │ contacts │      │ contacts │      │ contacts │
└──────────┘      └──────────┘      └──────────┘      └──────────┘
```
- **A Hero type** — a giant number/word is the design (sale, countdown, anniversary); the subject may overlap it.
- **B Split** — colour field one side with the type, subject the other (services, education, clinics).
- **C Frame/arch** — the subject inside a painted arch/circle, type around it (beauty, fashion, premium).
- **D Bottom-heavy** — image fills the top 60%, type block anchored low on a painted dark/colour area (food, events, travel).
- **E Centred icon** — product centred on a clean colour world, symmetric type (product launch, FMCG).
- **F Diagonal** — dynamic split or motion (sport, sale, energy).
- **G Grid/tiles** — 3–6 products or features in painted tiles (menu, catalogue, packages).
- **H Full-bleed mood** — one atmospheric image, minimal type (premium, concerts, real estate).
- **I Radial** — rays, rings or a burst painted from one focal point (the product, a face, a number); type sits between the rays or around the centre. Energy, launches, events.
- **J Axial** — one strong diagonal (or vertical) axis painted across the frame — a band, a beam of light, a road, a ribbon; the subject and the type hang off that axis. Type stays horizontal (the tool cannot rotate it); the axis carries the movement.
- **K Product + callouts** — the product dead centre and large; 2–4 short feature callouts (`small`, with icons) left and right of it at its height, headline above, price/offer and contacts below (tech, gadgets, cosmetics). A variant: headline and description in a left column, the product big on the right.

**Grid types behind these maps:** column grid (text on 1–2 shared column edges — most posters), modular grid (equal tiles — G), hierarchical grid (blocks sized by importance — A, D), radial (I), axial (J). Name the grid you use; every block must sit on it.

**Variety:** the brief lists the client's last posters (map, style, palette, type). Make each new one clearly different in map, style, palette and headline treatment — a feed of look-alikes is a failure. Report your choices in `deliver_poster`'s `design` so the next poster can differ from this one.

## 4b. Design formulas (systems, not guesses)
- **Type scale:** each step ≈ 1.25–1.6× the one below (small → subtitle → title → display). The tool's roles already follow a scale — don't fake a size between them by shrinking a title; pick the role.
- **Line height:** body lines 1.4–1.6× the size (the tool sets ~1.4); stacked headlines tight (~1.0–1.1).
- **Spacing:** margins and gaps are multiples of one unit (≈ 2% of the width): gap inside a group 1 unit, between groups 3–4 units, margins 3–4 units. Equal gaps for equal relations.
- **Contrast ratio:** small text ≥ 4.5:1 against what is behind it, large headlines ≥ 3:1.
- **Nested corners:** a pill inside a rounded card has a smaller radius (inner radius = outer − padding); never the same radius.
- **Optical alignment:** round shapes and triangles look smaller — let them overshoot the line slightly; trust the eye after the maths.
- **Consistency:** repeat the same choices for the same job (one button style, one icon style, one label style) — a system reads as professional.

## 5. Type on the picture (add_text)
- **Scale contrast:** the hero element (headline or number) is `display`/`title`, everything else `subtitle`/`small`. Two-tone accent on **one** word.
- **Alignment:** one alignment axis per poster (all left on the same margin, or all centred). Mixed alignments look amateur.
- **Grouping:** related lines sit tight together; groups are separated by clear space. Kicker → headline tight; headline → offer a clear gap; CTA alone.
- **Restraint:** plain type on calm areas (use `scrim` for photos) beats boxes. Use a filled shape only where it adds meaning (the deal, the button).
- **Benefits:** ≤ 3 short lines, or turn them into one strong line. A checklist is for services, not for every poster.
- Exact strings and fonts: `poster-typography`; panels/chips: `poster-layouts`.

## 6. Colour
Palette from the logo / brand / packaging, as hex. Max 2 main + 1 accent; the accent is used once or twice (offer, CTA). Type colour contrasts strongly with what is behind it. Mood: premium → dark/neutral + one jewel tone; food → warm; youth/sport → saturated, hard light; family → warm, soft; health → clean whites with one confident brand colour, not pale blue everywhere. Style recipes: `poster-styles`; avoiding generic looks: `poster-taste`.

## 7. Logo
Placed last with `add_logo` (the client's file, pixel-exact) — size, corner, clear space per **`logo-placement`**. In the visual prompt: "no logos", and keep a calm corner. Never invent a logo; without one the brand name may be a `small` text line.

## 8. Quality bar — check the result before delivering
1. **Squint test:** blur your eyes — one shape dominates, and it is the hero; the reading path is obvious.
2. **2-second test:** the hook and the offer are understood in 2 seconds.
3. **Count the boxes:** ≤ 2 filled shapes.
4. **Count the sizes:** ≤ 4 text sizes; the headline clearly the largest.
5. **Alignment and balance:** everything on one axis or a deliberate grid; the hero on a thirds point (or symmetric by intent); the two halves balance.
6. **Colour:** one named scheme, the accent used once or twice, every line in strong contrast.
7. **Air:** real space around the headline and the hero; no stray badges in the corners.
8. **Does it look designed?** Would it pass as an agency post for a real brand — or as a template flyer? If a template, change the visual (§3) or the map (§4), not just the text.
9. Nothing covers the face or the product; no dead hole in the layout.

## 9. Formats and a matching set
Formats are aspect ratios (`4:5`, `1:1`, `9:16`, `16:9` … or `auto`); the file is centre-cropped (short side 1080 px). Keep subject and text in the middle 80% — the middle 60% vertically for 9:16. Several ratios → `format-composition`.
1. Make and approve the most important ratio first (usually `4:5`).
2. Other ratios in **one turn** (they run in parallel): same model, the approved clean visual (+ product) as references — "same design, scene and colour world, re-laid-out for 16:9: subject left, type right".
3. Re-check the text of every ratio; `deliver_poster` each with its own image.
