# Food & drink

## Keep the real dish
If the client attached a photo of the dish, use `gemini` + reference and phrase it as an edit — new light, surface and props, the food itself unchanged. Customers will compare the ad with what they get.

## Angles
- **Overhead (90°)** — bowls, pizza, platters, flat lays of several dishes, tables of shared food.
- **45°** — plated dishes, burgers, cakes, most restaurant food.
- **Eye level (0°)** — drinks, stacked items (burgers, pancakes, layered desserts), anything with height.

## Appetite cues
- Light from the side or back (window light), never flat frontal; warm tones for comfort food, bright daylight for fresh/healthy.
- Steam on hot dishes, condensation on cold drinks, a glossy sauce, a crumb or two, a bite taken.
- Fresh garnish and raw ingredients around the dish (herbs, spices, a cut lemon) — only ingredients the dish really contains.
- Dark surfaces (slate, dark wood) make colourful food pop; light marble / linen for brunch, bakery and healthy.
- Leave some imperfection — perfectly symmetric food looks fake.

## Mongolian dishes
Buuz, khuushuur, tsuivan, bansh, khorkhog, suutei tsai, boortsog, aaruul: show them in honest homely styling — stoneware or traditional bowls, wooden boards, felt or ornament textiles as accents (see `mongolian-culture`), never plastic plates. Steam is essential for buuz and tsai.

## Drinks & coffee
Glass with condensation, ice and a garnish; coffee with latte art, a saucer and a spoon, beans or a pastry as a prop; backlight makes drinks glow.

## Offers & menus
Show value with abundance: a combo laid out together, a full table for a family set, two drinks for a 1+1 offer. No prices or text in the image unless the client asks.

## Food reels
Hook shots: cheese pull, sauce pour, steam rising, a slow push-in on a sizzling pan, pouring a drink over ice, cutting into a dessert. 4–6 s clips, one action each; end on the full dish or the table with the logo still.
