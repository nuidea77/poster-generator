# Interiors & real estate

## Keep the real place
If the client sent photos of the venue or building, it must stay recognisable: use `gemini` + references to improve light, tidy, stage and re-time the photo ("same room, same furniture and layout; evening warm light; tidy; plants added") — never invent a different room and present it as theirs. For a project not built yet, a clearly rendered 3D look is acceptable.

## Interior photography
- 16–24 mm wide angle at chest height, verticals straight (no tilted walls), one-point perspective for symmetric rooms, a corner view to show depth.
- Light: mix of daylight from windows and warm lamps; "blue hour" outside the window for evenings.
- Staging: a few lived-in details (coffee cup, open book, plants, throw blanket); no clutter, no people or one soft-focus person to give scale.
- Venues: show the atmosphere the customer buys — a full café in warm light, a gym with morning sun on equipment, a hotel bed with crisp linen.

## Exteriors & buildings
- Golden hour or blue hour with interior lights on; clean sky; Ulaanbaatar context (mountains on the horizon, Bogd Khan, snow in winter) where it helps — no recognisable landmarks that would mislead about the location.
- Aerial / drone view for new districts and townhouses; eye-level hero view with landscaping for apartment blocks.
- Do not add amenities, parks or views the property does not have (see `ad-compliance`).

## Ratios
Interiors and buildings suit **16:9, 3:2, 4:5**; for 9:16 stories use a vertical corner view or a tall element (staircase, window wall).

## Walkthrough reels
Slow gimbal-like moves: push-in through the door, lateral truck along the living room, pan to the window view, tilt up the building façade, drone pull-back over the complex. 5–10 s clips (`seedance` handles long steady moves well); end on the façade or the venue sign plus the logo still.
