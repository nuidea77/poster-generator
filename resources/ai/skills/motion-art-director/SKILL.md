# Motion art director

Core principle: **motion is narrative, not decoration.** Every shot exists for a reason, enters at a reason, and moves for a reason. Before choosing any camera move, ask: "where must the viewer look at this moment?"

## 1. One message

What should the viewer remember and do after 90 seconds? If it does not fit one sentence, the reel will be vague. Write it down before planning shots.

## 2. Motion metaphor

The movement grows out of the message:

| Message | Motion |
|---|---|
| growth, progress | rising, crane up, things growing |
| opening, launch | doors/curtains opening, light switching on, reveal from darkness |
| connection, community | people coming together, lines meeting, hands passing something |
| reliability, premium | steady slow dolly, calm orbit, no shake |
| energy, youth, sport | handheld follow, fast push-ins, splash, speed |
| freshness, food | steam, pour, slow-motion drops, macro texture |

A reel whose motion has nothing to do with the message is just moving wallpaper.

## 3. Rhythm across 90 seconds

- **The first 1–2 seconds decide.** Open with the strongest image, never with the logo (the logo ends the reel). The first frame is never empty or black.
- **One shot, one idea.** Do not ask one clip to show the product, the venue and the people at once.
- **Breathing.** Alternate intense shots with calmer ones (e.g. fast hook → slow world → medium desire → slow hero). Constant intensity tires; constant calm bores.
- **Hold the ending.** The final shot (logo end card or hero) is calm and readable — at least 3–5 seconds of stillness-like motion.
- **Understandable without sound.** Reels autoplay muted; the pictures alone must tell the story.

## 4. Continuity and transitions

- Carry one thing across neighbouring cuts: a colour, a shape, a direction (if shot A moves up, shot B enters from below), or a matching subject position.
- Keep one look for the whole reel: same palette, light quality and grade, written as one style line repeated in every clip prompt.
- Vary scale and camera move between neighbours (wide → close, push-in → orbit) so cuts feel intentional; never the same move twice in a row.
- No flashing or strobing (more than 3 flashes per second is a seizure risk).

## 5. Vertical safe areas (Reels, Stories, TikTok)

1080×1920. Platform buttons and captions cover the top ≈14%, the bottom ≈35% and the right edge. Keep the product, faces and the logo in the central band. Instagram's grid shows only the middle 3:4 of a reel's cover frame — the first shot should read there too.

## 6. Mongolian limits (not legal advice, 2026)

- **State symbols** (state emblem, flag, Soyombo as a state mark) must not be used in commercial advertising.
- **Advertising law**: tobacco advertising is banned; alcohol is tightly restricted; using a real person's name, image or words needs permission; content disparaging state symbols, historical figures, religion or banknotes is prohibited.
- **Cultural visuals**: do not improvise the Soyombo, traditional ornaments or Mongolian script calligraphy — models distort them. Use them only when the client supplies the artwork (see `mongolian-culture`).
